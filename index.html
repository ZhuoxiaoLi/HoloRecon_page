01 / OVERVIEWPROBLEM & KEY INSIGHT

Local geometry as evidence
for global consistency.

Sparse mapping anchors predicted geometry.
Predicted geometry verifies distant revisits.

Long-range panoramic reconstruction requires detailed geometry and globally consistent camera poses. Repetitive architecture and limited overlap make distant revisits difficult to verify, while feed-forward models provide local geometry within bounded inference windows.

HoloRecon couples an ORB-based sparse panoramic mapping frontend with a fine-tuned Pi3 model. Sparse landmarks calibrate predicted depths; the resulting geometry supports 3D verification of feature correspondences across distant revisits. Verified connections drive pose-graph optimization, landmark fusion, and GPU-accelerated panoramic bundle adjustment. Cached dense predictions are then updated using corrected camera poses and recalibrated depths, without repeating model inference.

01 / ANCHOR

Calibrate local geometry

An ORB-based ERP frontend estimates poses and sparse landmarks. Pi3, fine-tuned on Holo360D, predicts local geometry from perspective views. Landmark-supported window scales and per-frame corrections anchor these depths to the sparse map.

02 / VERIFY

Establish long-range constraints

Spatial overlap and visibility propose candidate submaps. Depth-supported ORB correspondences estimate a common Sim(3) transform, verified across multiple keyframes using bidirectional distance and bearing consistency.

03 / REFINE

Update sparse and dense maps

Pose-graph correction and landmark fusion establish cross-region observations for GPU-accelerated panoramic bundle adjustment. Corrected source-camera poses and refitted depth scales update cached points without rerunning Pi3.

GEOMETRY-SUPPORTED LOOP VERIFICATION

Verify a shared transform
across multiple views.

Similar facades can produce plausible image matches at different locations. HoloRecon checks whether one geometric transform explains correspondences across multiple keyframes on both sides of a revisit.

Large corrections receive additional validation using separate frame groups and neighboring historical submaps before their camera constraints enter global optimization.

Implementation

16 panoramic keyframes per submap, with 4 shared keyframes between consecutive submaps. Experiments use a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Workstation Edition GPU.
